## Goal
Make the block something the engineer talks their way into, not a YAML form they fill in. This is **example mapping** — start from the rule, draw out concrete examples, and let the questions that surface become findings rather than silent assumptions.

Add the elicitation turn to the two authoring paths that already elicit-then-confirm — `add.md` at its existing "show the draft and confirm" step, and `implement.md` section 2 alongside the plan. No new flag, no new entry point.

The turn, in order:

1. **Name the actor and the outcome.** Reuse the Value story already in the template — `As a <role> I want <capability> so that <benefit>` is the feature narrative; do not ask for it twice.
2. **Elicit the happy path** as one Given/When/Then, and confirm it.
3. **Probe, one at a time, confirming each before moving on** — what should happen when it **fails**; what happens at the **edge** (empty, first-run, maximum, concurrent); who is **not allowed** to do this. One question per turn, never a batch.
4. **Ask what proves it.** For each confirmed scenario, ask for the command — or accept "don't know yet" and leave `by:` empty, which the T1 lint check will surface as a WARNING rather than blocking.
5. **Stop when the answers stop changing.** Two probes that add nothing new ends the turn. Do not exhaust the taxonomy.

Where 1.20 has shipped, the spec reviewer's SMART/Measurable findings seed this directly — it names which criterion lacks a proof, and the elicitation turn is where the engineer supplies it.

## Done when
- [ ] `add.md` and `implement.md` section 2 both run the elicitation turn at their existing confirm points, with no new flag or entry point.
- [ ] The turn asks one question at a time and confirms each scenario before the next — proven by a transcript showing sequential probes, not a batched list.
- [ ] Declining the turn writes the spec exactly as today, with no `scenarios:` block.
- [ ] "Don't know yet" is accepted and leaves `by:` empty; `/flow:lint --specs` then reports it as a WARNING.
- [ ] The turn does not re-ask for the Value story when one is already drafted.
- [ ] Two consecutive probes that surface nothing new end the turn — proven by a run against a trivially simple spec that stops after the happy path plus two probes.

## Notes
Depends on T1; better after T2. The failure mode to design against is interrogation: a fixed checklist of eight probes on a two-line spec makes `--add` slower than writing the spec by hand, which is exactly what the toolkit is for.
